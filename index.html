Frequently asked questions

A little more
context.

How PUKNO works today, where personal memory is going and how we're thinking about privacy along the way.

What does PUKNO remember?

PUKNO begins by remembering the activity across your Mac — what apps you used, what you were working on and when it happened. It turns that activity into a searchable timeline that helps you return to things you may no longer remember exactly.

Does PUKNO work offline?

Yes. Today, PUKNO runs and processes your activity locally on your Mac. Core activity capture, history and local recall can work without sending your personal context to a remote AI service.

In future versions, you may optionally connect PUKNO to your own AI workstation or use PUKNO Private Compute for tasks that benefit from more powerful models.

Does my data leave my device?

PBy default, PUKNO is local-first. Your activity history, memory and personal context are designed to remain on your devices.

Future capabilities such as PUKNO Sync and PUKNO Private Compute are being designed to extend what PUKNO can do without giving PUKNO unrestricted access to your personal data. Remote processing would only happen when required for a feature you choose to use and under the privacy controls designed for that service.

Can PUKNO take actions for me?

Not yet. PUKNO is starting with memory, context and reasoning. Future PUKNO Agents are intended to go beyond answering questions by understanding what you want to accomplish, choosing the tools they need and carrying out the steps for you.

That could mean finding information you saw before, working with files, navigating apps, creating calendar events, sending messages or completing multi-step tasks across your Mac and connected services.

The difference is context: PUKNO Agents are intended to act using the personal memory PUKNO has already built, so you can ask things like “Book the hotel I was looking at last week” without having to explain everything again.

Which platforms are supported?

macOS
PUKNO is currently being built for macOS, with the first releases focused on Apple silicon Macs. The macOS version is the primary development target and the first platform where PUKNO's activity capture, local context and memory features are being developed.

Windows
Windows support is planned. PUKNO will use native Windows system and accessibility APIs to understand which applications, windows and workflows you're interacting with, allowing it to build the same kind of private activity context and personal memory available on macOS.

Linux
Linux support is also planned, with desktop-specific integrations rather than a single generic implementation. Initial targets include GNOME and COSMIC Desktop, with support shaped around the APIs and security model exposed by each desktop environment and compositor.

Across every platform, the goal is the same: capture only the activity signals needed to build your personal context and PUKNO Memory while respecting the security, privacy and permission model of each operating system.

What is available now, and what is coming next?

Available today

PUKNO Activity Capture
PUKNO currently captures and organizes activity from your Mac — including the apps you use, application and window context, sessions, timestamps and supported web activity. This creates a private activity history of what you were doing and when, forming the foundation that PUKNO Memory will build on.

This activity layer is the foundation for everything that comes next. Instead of starting with an empty AI conversation, PUKNO begins building a history of the activity and context that make up your digital life.

What we're building toward

PUKNO Memory
PUKNO Memory will transform captured activity into meaningful, connected personal context. Instead of only knowing that you opened an app or visited a website, PUKNO will work to understand what you were doing — the topics, people, places, projects, media and moments involved.

Over time, related activity can become connected memories that PUKNO can retrieve, reconstruct and reason over. This means you could ask questions based on partial memories such as “What was that hotel I looked at a few months ago?” without remembering the exact website, date or filename.

PUKNO Sync
PUKNO Sync is intended to make your memory, context and personal intelligence available across your devices while keeping that information private. Your activity, memories and personal context would be protected with end-to-end encryption so that only your authorized devices can access them.

The synchronization layer is intended to move encrypted data, not understand it. Future PUKNO's sync should not need access to the readable contents of your memory for your devices to stay synchronized and continue building the same personal context.

PUKNO Private Compute
PUKNO is designed to keep personal context and AI processing local whenever possible. Some requests, however, may benefit from models that are too large or computationally demanding to run efficiently on your device.

PUKNO Private Compute is intended to provide a privacy-preserving path to that additional computing power. When you explicitly approve a request, PUKNO could prepare only the context required for that task and send it to an isolated confidential-compute environment running a more capable model.

The goal is to give PUKNO access to powerful remote inference when needed without exposing your broader personal memory to the infrastructure providing that compute.

This could eventually be provided through PUKNO-operated infrastructure or compatible confidential-compute services. It would be invoked only when needed, only with your explicit consent and always under your control. Only the context required for the task you approve would be sent. Data would be encrypted in transit and at rest and processed only inside the protected compute environment.

PUKNO Agents
PUKNO Agents are intended to turn memory, context and reasoning into action. An agent could understand what you want to accomplish, retrieve the relevant personal context, decide which tools are needed and carry out multiple steps across your computer, apps and connected services.

Instead of only answering “What hotel was I looking at?”, a PUKNO Agent could eventually understand “Book the hotel I was looking at last week,” recover the relevant memory, identify the hotel you meant, choose the appropriate tools and complete the task on your behalf.
